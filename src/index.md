## Separate Ways, frame by frame

Gabriel posted a short video of the synth part from Journey's Separate Ways (Worlds Apart), the song that opens the album Frontiers, released as a single in January 1983. Three stacked frames of the same take: the keyboard from above, Gabriel at the desk with the mixing console, the screen and the rack behind him, and the synthesizer's panel. He explained it by voice on 2 November 2026, and every claim was checked. [2]

> "I put this video to show how it works." (Gabriel, 2 November 2026) [2]

The signal chain: [2]

1. **Keyboard:** a small Bluetooth MIDI keyboard, no cable. The one in the video matches CME's Xkey Air.
2. **Synthesizer:** a Behringer Pro-800, an eight-voice reproduction of the Sequential Prophet-600 from 1982.
3. **Chorus:** to widen the sound, as on the 1983 record.
4. **Delay:** to repeat it.

**The trick at the end:** while playing, he turns the decay down, so each chord stops almost at once (staccato), and the delay repeats the short chord, each repeat softer than the last. [2]

**Why the keyboard does not lag:** Bluetooth headphones stream buffered audio. MIDI over Bluetooth Low Energy sends only small note messages, and its specification requires the connection to update every 15 milliseconds or less. In Gabriel's words, "there's a different protocol if you want to connect MIDI." [2]

**What the checking corrected** (he flagged both himself: "They used that Prophet 5, I think. I have to check that out."): the album's credits list a Roland Jupiter-8 for the opening track, not a Prophet-5; and the Pro-800 copies the Prophet-600, a later and cheaper Sequential instrument than the Prophet-5. [2]

The video is on his YouTube channel, https://www.youtube.com/@gabrieldacruz_composer. A direct link: TO CONFIRM.

## One player, three old iPhones, no crew

The three frames are one take. He bought two iPhone 12s and an iPhone 13 second-hand, "because they were cheaper, and I just needed the camera", and connected them wirelessly to an iPad running Final Cut Pro. He found the feature by accident: "When I discovered this, I was blown away." [3]

- The feature is **Live Multicam**, released by Apple on 20 June 2024 in Final Cut Pro for iPad 2.0, with a free app for the phones, Final Cut Camera. [3]
- Up to four angles; the iPad's own camera can be one of them. [3]
- The iPad shows every angle live, in sync, and controls each camera's settings. [3]
- Each phone records its own full-quality file, which transfers to the iPad afterwards. [3]
- The project then moves to Final Cut Pro on the Mac for the edit. [3]
- Apple lists the iPhone XS or later for Final Cut Camera (as read on 2 November 2026). [3]
- Good for: a musician's video, an interview ("like a live TV situation", in his words), a lesson or masterclass, a small live show. [3]

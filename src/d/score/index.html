II. Separate Ways allegro, then staccato

A synth video, explained frame by frame

Journey's Separate Ways (Worlds Apart) opens the album Frontiers, released as a single in January 1983. Gabriel played its synth part in his studio and filmed it from three cameras in one take. Then he explained it, by voice, and every claim was checked.2

"I put this video to show how it works."Gabriel, 2 November 20262

The trick at the end: he turns the decay down while playing, so each chord goes staccato, and the delay repeats the short chord. The checking corrected two details he had flagged himself: the record is credited to a Roland Jupiter-8, and his Pro-800 copies the Prophet-600, not the Prophet-5.2

III. Three cameras con sorpresa

Three old iPhones, an iPad, no crew

Two iPhone 12s and an iPhone 13, bought second-hand "because they were cheaper, and I just needed the camera", connected wirelessly to an iPad running Final Cut Pro. Apple released the feature, Live Multicam, on 20 June 2024 in Final Cut Pro for iPad 2.0, with a free camera app for the phones. Up to four angles, the iPad's own camera can be one, each phone keeps its own full-quality file, and the project finishes in Final Cut Pro on the Mac.3

"When I discovered this, I was blown away."Gabriel, who found it by accident3

V. His path as told by Dinis

Lisbon, the Algarve, London

As told by Dinis

In his brother's telling: a band in the Algarve first, Gabriel with Dinis on drums, about twelve gigs a week. Then songs, a music school, a studio built around a Mac Studio, Logic and Pro Tools, and, in Lisbon, an orchestra he put together to play film music live, in time with the films on a screen. Composer, arranger, conductor, producer, studio technician and teacher.1

The band's name and years, the school, and the concert's name, venue and dates are to confirm, and the steps after the band are in no confirmed order.

Side B: how it was made

Separate Ways, explained

Journey's Separate Ways (Worlds Apart) opens the album Frontiers, a single in January 1983. Gabriel played its synth part in his studio, filmed it from three cameras in one take, and explained it by voice; every claim was checked.2

"I put this video to show how it works."Gabriel, 2 November 20262

At the end he turns the decay down while playing: each chord goes staccato, and the delay repeats it. The checking corrected two details he flagged himself: the record is credited to a Roland Jupiter-8, and his Pro-800 copies the Prophet-600.2

Three cameras, no crew

Two iPhone 12s and an iPhone 13, bought second-hand "because they were cheaper, and I just needed the camera", connected wirelessly to an iPad running Final Cut Pro. Live Multicam, released by Apple on 20 June 2024: up to four angles, live and in sync, full-quality files from each phone, finished in Final Cut Pro on the Mac.3

"When I discovered this, I was blown away."Gabriel, who found it by accident3

Liner notes, as told by Dinis

His brother remembers a band in the Algarve first, Gabriel with Dinis on drums, about twelve gigs a week. Then songs, a music school, a studio built around a Mac Studio, Logic and Pro Tools, and, in Lisbon, an orchestra he put together to play film music live, in time with the films on a screen. He can take a song, write it out for a full orchestra, and then stand in front of that orchestra.1

The band's name and years, the school, and the concert's name, venue and dates are to confirm; the steps after the band are in no confirmed order.
